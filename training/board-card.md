# BOARD CARD — keep this open while playing

Short on purpose. Everything here is something I can actually do on a 10+5
clock. The old every-move "Sweep" was retired: 190 games after it was
introduced, my move quality didn't improve (`docs/08-what-the-data-says.md`).
These are **triggers**, not procedures for every move.

---

## Before the game

- **Nothing on screen but the board.** No video, no audio, no second tab.
  My long thinks with something else on screen were distraction, not
  calculation.
- **Notepad and pen next to the keyboard.**

---

## TRIGGER 1 — I'm winning (+3 or better, or I just won material)

This is where my games are lost. Before touching a piece, out loud:

> **"Mate? — every check — every capture."**

1. **Mate.** Is there a mate in one? In two?
2. **Every check.** Name each one. For each: *does it mate?*
3. **Every capture.** Name each one.
4. Only then consider a quiet move.
5. **Before releasing ANY move here: their checks? their captures?**
   67 of 109 middlegame collapses in my last 141 analysed games were
   punished by the opponent's very next move — a check or a capture.

**A free piece will still be free after I've looked for mate. The mate might
not still be there.**

## TRIGGER 2 — I see a good forcing move while calculating

**Write it down immediately** (three characters: `Qf4+`). Then keep
thinking. Before releasing any move, look at the note. If I'm about to play
something else, say why out loud.

## TRIGGER 3 — they just moved

One question first: **"What does their move attack?"** Trace the line the
piece opened.

## TRIGGER 4 — I'm about to capture or recapture

- Two pieces can recapture? **Name what their most active piece hits after
  each option** before choosing.
- Recapture near my king with a **PIECE**, not a pawn.
- Before any capture: **what does it allow?**

---

## HARD RULES

- Piece attacked → **count its safe squares before reacting.** Two or more
  safe squares = not a crisis. Look for an escape that also threatens.
- (As White) my bishop on b5 hit by `...a6` → **`Bxc6` is the default.**
  That knight defends e5 and d4; judge a trade by the piece's jobs, not
  its value. Retreating is the move I have to justify.
- Endgame with few pieces → the mate check matters **most** here, not least.
- Endgame, I'm better → **king to the centre first.** After a check, step
  toward where the king works. Pawns on the opposite colour to my bishop.
- Pawn storm on my wing → **answer in the centre.** Don't castle into it.

---

## Opening reminders (Black: Caro-Kann / Slav)

- Move 2 vs 1.e4: `...d5`, almost always — even vs an early queen.
- Light-squared bishop out **before** `...e6`.
- Classical: `4...Bf5`, never `4...Nd7` (`5.Qe2 Ngf6?? 6.Nd6#`).
- Exchange: `...a6` **before** `...b5`.
- Jobava/London with `Nb5`: guard c7 with `...Na6`.
