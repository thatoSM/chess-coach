# Wellbeing & Schedule

Most of what this file used to claim was tested and failed (late-night play,
gaming drain, fatigue, sleep, tilt, volume). The data is in
`docs/08-what-the-data-says.md`. What's left here is what I actually keep.

---

## The rules I keep

1. **Chess first in a session, or not at all.** My own choice — I play rated
   chess with a clean head rather than squeezing it in after something else.
   It's a preference, not a proven rating effect.
2. **Nothing on screen but the board** while playing. This one IS backed by
   data: in a stretch where I had video running mid-game, my 20-second-plus
   thinks had 18–22% error rates vs 7–8% for quicker moves, and they
   weren't reactions to threats — they were distraction.
3. **No volume cap and no stop-after-losses rule.** Both were retired: games
   13+ in a session were my best band, and my error rate after two losses
   didn't change.

## Rules that were retired, and why

| Rule | Why it went |
|---|---|
| "No rated chess late at night" | I've never played rated rapid after 23:00. Nothing to fix. |
| "No rated chess after League" | Never shown in the data. |
| "Stop after two losses" | Tilt isn't measurable in my games. |
| "Max 3 games a day" | Games 13+ of a session were my best. |
| Sleep prescriptions | The worse-sleep session produced the better accuracy. |

**Why they failed:** 75–85% of my performance variance is within a single
day. Day-level habits can't explain most of it. Per-move habits can.

---

## Breaks

Between games, 2–5 minutes away from the screen is enough. After a bad loss
or a long block of games, 15–30 minutes. These are for comfort and focus,
not a rating rule.

---

## What I log per game (the experiment fields)

- **Tea:** yes / no (1 cup of Trinco, ~1 hour before).
- **Before chess:** what I was doing (e.g. Fortnite, studying, nothing).
- **Focus:** 1–5, my honest rating before the first move.

The tea comparison runs after **20+ games each side**. Compare score against
rating expectation and worst-move size, not just win rate. A gap under ~10
points on 20 games each is noise. Given the within-day variance above, the
most likely result is no difference — and that's a useful answer too.

---

## The distinction that matters

**Not every bad game is a tired game.** Game 5 was 57% accuracy with six
blunders and I was completely fresh. If I blame the wrong cause, I fix the
wrong thing.
