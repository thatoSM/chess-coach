# Wellbeing & Schedule

Leak #5 is a **state** problem, not a skill problem — which makes it the
cheapest leak to fix. I don't have to get better at anything. I just have to
stop playing rated chess when I'm not in a condition to run the scan.

> **CONFIRM:** the break tiers and priority order below are a reasonable
> structure but partly reconstructed. Adjust them to what actually fits my
> days.

---

## The core rule

> **Chess goes FIRST in a session, or not at all.**

Not "chess after one more game." First. When my working memory is clean.

**No rated chess after a long gaming session.** This is the single rule that,
if I keep it, saves me the most rating for the least effort.

---

## Why gaming specifically drains chess

Chess and competitive gaming compete for the same resource: **sustained working
memory under emotional load.**

| Activity | Drain | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **League of Legends** | **Heaviest** | Locked-in 30–40 min matches, constant map tracking, teammate friction, no clean exit point |
| **Fortnite** | Moderate | Shorter rounds, but high arousal and fast decision-cycling |
| Casual / single-player | Light | Stoppable, low stakes |

League is the one that reliably precedes rating dips. It's not that gaming is
bad — it's that after League, the part of my brain that runs "checks, captures,
threats" is already spent, and I fall straight back onto the memorised script.
That's Leak #2 and Leak #1 arriving together.

---

## Break tiers — matching the break to the drain

After chess, or between chess and anything else:

**Tier 1 — Micro (2–5 min), between games**
Stand up. Water. Look out of a window at something far away. No screen.
Enough to reset attention between two games.

**Tier 2 — Short (15–30 min), after 2–3 games or a bad loss**
Leave the desk entirely. Walk, eat something, music, shower. No competitive
input. This is the tier that stops tilt from compounding.

**Tier 3 — Long (2+ hours or next day), after a heavy session**
This is the buffer between League and chess, and it should be a real gap — not
"I finished at 11pm and I'll play one quick rapid." If the gap isn't there,
chess doesn't happen. Puzzles instead.

**The honest test:** if I'm reaching for chess *because* I'm restless after
gaming, that's the exact state where I lose rating. That feeling is not
readiness.

---

## Priority order within a session

When I sit down with time to spend, this is the order:

```
1. PUZZLES        15–20 min, slow, Harder difficulty
2. RATED CHESS    2–3 games max, while fresh
3. ANALYSIS       10 min on whichever game went worst — name the leak, log it
4. GAMING         after all of the above, or on a non-chess day
```

Everything above the line I do while sharp. Gaming is below the line because
it doesn't need me sharp, and it costs the thing chess needs.

## Volume
   - No cap. Games 13+ of a session are my best games (63% score,
     0.086 error rate across 41 games).
   - Losses don't compound: error rate after 2 straight losses is
     0.117 vs 0.113 otherwise. Tilt is not a measured problem for me.
   - Rating is a measurement, not a possession. Judge over 20+ games.

## Sleep and time of day

Late-night rapid is where the blunder-brawls live. My best games have been
daytime games with a clear head.

If it's late and I want chess: **puzzles.** They're stoppable mid-solve, the
rating is a thermometer I don't care about protecting, and they still train the
exact thing I need. There is no version of "I'll just play one rated game" at
midnight that has gone well.

---

## The distinction that matters

**Not every bad game is a tired game.**

Game 5 was 57% accuracy with six blunders and I was completely fresh. If I
blame fatigue for a game that was actually "didn't run the scan," I fix my
sleep schedule instead of my routine and nothing improves.

So when logging a game, **record my state honestly** — fresh / tired / after
gaming / late night. It's a field in the template for a reason. Over time it
tells me which leak I'm actually dealing with.

---

## The point of all this

I'm not optimising my life around chess. I'm removing the two or three
predictable situations where I hand away rating I earned. That's it. Everything
else — gaming included — stays.
